How the unit treats the stream

An atmospheric horizontal vessel with six cells. Gas is dissolved into a recycle stream under pressure and released as fine bubbles in the four central cells. The bubbles attach to oil droplets and solids and carry them to the surface, where they are skimmed off.

  1. Inlet and stilling

    Wastewater enters the first cell. Flow is calmed and free oil begins to separate.

  2. Gas saturation

    A dual-sided impeller pump draws in water and gas together and holds the gas in solution under back pressure.

  3. Bubble release

    The saturated stream crosses a control valve. The pressure drop releases bubbles averaging about 20 microns into cells 2 to 5.

  4. Skim and discharge

    Oil rises and is skimmed to the oil pump. Treated water leaves through the final cell.

Why bubble size matters

Smaller bubbles attach to the underside of oil droplets and lift them faster. Dissolved gas flotation produces far more fine bubbles than induced gas flotation, which improves oil capture and reduces the chemical dose needed.

Automated level and skim control reduces operator attention. Redundant pumps keep the unit on line during pump maintenance.

Chemical use

Fine, evenly distributed bubbles improve oil capture and lower the flotation chemical dose compared with induced gas units.
